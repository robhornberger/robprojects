The players in the US drug pricing chain
🧪
Manufacturer
Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly
Creates the drug, sets list price (WAC), pays rebates to PBMs. Only keeps 25–40% of list price.
🚚
Wholesaler
McKesson, AmerisourceBergen, Cardinal
Physically moves drugs from factory to pharmacy. 3 companies control ~90% of distribution. Thin margins (~2–4%).
🏦
PBM
CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, OptumRx
Negotiates rebates with manufacturers, decides formulary placement, processes claims. Keeps portion of rebates — amount is often secret.
🛡️
Insurer
Blue Cross, Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealth
Pays pharmacy reimbursement, receives rebate pass-through from PBM. Sets patient copay tiers and coverage rules.
💊
Pharmacy
CVS, Walgreens, independent
Dispenses drug to patient, collects copay. Often reimbursed at rates below purchase cost for specialty drugs. Squeezed margins.
👤
Patient
260M US adults
Pays copay ($0–$1,349 depending on insurance). Uninsured see full list price. Only 20–25% of commercial plans cover GLP-1s for weight loss.
Why the list price is artificially inflated

The $1,349 list price exists because the entire system runs on percentage-based rebates. Every middleman's cut is a percentage of the list price. If the PBM negotiates a 55% rebate, that's $742 going back. If Novo set the list at $500 (closer to what they keep), a 55% rebate would only be $275 — and the PBM wouldn't give Wegovy favorable formulary placement.

It's a system where the sticker price is inflated so everyone can claim they got a big discount. The manufacturer appears to charge $1,349. The PBM tells the insurer it "saved" them $742. The insurer tells the employer it's managing costs. The uninsured patient who has no one negotiating on their behalf sees $1,349 and either can't afford the drug or goes into debt.

This is precisely why the DTC/TrumpRx model at $350/month is disruptive: it bypasses the entire chain. Novo keeps ~$333 net (vs ~$340–500 traditional), the patient pays 74% less, and the middlemen get nothing. Everyone wins except PBMs and wholesalers — which is why those entities are fighting the model aggressively through lobbying and contract restrictions.

Why this matters for investors
Bullish signals
DTC channels improve unit economics. Price cuts don't hurt manufacturer net revenue as much as headlines suggest. Volume growth outpaces price decline. Generic entry is still 5+ years away. Oral formulations open massive new market.
Bearish signals
Medicare negotiation will compress net prices. IRA provisions directly target semaglutide. Generic entry by ~2031 could crater branded pricing. Political pressure intensifying. PBM reform could restructure entire rebate system.
